FAQ

Digital marketing questions people ask.

How much should a small business spend on digital marketing?

Start with a small test budget you can afford to lose, run it for two to four weeks, and judge it by the cost of each real enquiry, not likes or clicks. Then put more money only into what works. For one dress manufacturer in Kerala, a ₹1,500 Meta Ads test brought 60+ enquiries and about ₹6 lakh in wholesale sales.

Which is better for a small business: Facebook ads or Google ads?

It depends on whether your customers are already searching. Google Ads works best when people search for what you offer, like "dentist near me". Facebook and Instagram ads work better for reaching people before they search, with video and helpful information. Many businesses end up using both. I compare them in Meta Ads vs Google Ads for clinics in Kerala.

How long does digital marketing take to show results?

Ads can bring enquiries within days, but give a campaign two to four weeks of data before you judge it. SEO usually takes three to six months for steady results. Social media growth comes from posting consistently for months, not weeks.

Should I hire a freelancer or a digital marketing agency?

If you need advice, a plan, or one or two channels managed, a freelancer or a consultant is usually enough and costs less. If you need many channels plus design and content every week, an agency with a full team can make sense. I explain how to choose in freelance digital marketer vs agency in Kerala.

Is SEO still worth it now that people use ChatGPT and Google AI?

Yes. Google says optimising for its AI Overviews and AI Mode is still SEO, because they use the normal search index. Clear pages that answer real questions, a complete Google Business Profile and genuine reviews help in both normal results and AI answers. More in how to get your business recommended by ChatGPT and Google AI Mode.

Can doctors and clinics in India advertise on social media?

Yes, but within strict rules. Under the NMC advertising guidelines issued on 6 October 2026, doctors can't use patient testimonials, before-and-after photos, discounts or "best" claims. Factual, educational content is allowed. Read the full breakdown in NMC advertising guidelines 2026.
