Meta Ads vs Google Ads for clinics in Kerala: which should you start with?

S Mohammed Safwan
17 October 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer: start with Google Ads if patients already search for your treatment (dental pain, ENT, physiotherapy, diagnostics). Start with Meta Ads if your treatment is planned and needs trust before someone books (hair, skin, cosmetic dentistry, fertility, wellness). Whichever you pick, set up tracking first, or you won't know which one is actually working.

Update, 20 October 2026: the NMC issued new advertising guidelines for doctors, clinics and hospitals on 6 October 2026. Testimonials, before-and-after photos, discounts, superiority claims and patient solicitation are now clearly restricted. Read my full guide to the NMC advertising guidelines 2026 before running any healthcare campaign.

"Should we do Facebook ads or Google ads?" is one of the first questions clinic owners ask me. It's the right question, but most people try to answer it by comparing the platforms. The better way is to look at your patients.

The one question that decides it: is the patient already searching?

Google and Meta reach people at completely different moments.

  • Google Ads shows your clinic to someone who is already looking. They typed "dentist near me", "best skin clinic in Kozhikode" or "physiotherapy Palakkad". The need exists. You're competing to be the one they call.
  • Meta Ads (Facebook and Instagram) shows your clinic to someone who is not looking yet but fits your ideal patient. They're scrolling. Your ad has to create interest and trust from zero.

So the real question isn't "which platform is better?" It's "do my patients search for this treatment when they need it, or do they need to be convinced first?"

When Google Ads usually makes more sense

Google tends to work better for treatments where the need is urgent, specific or clearly felt:

  • Dental emergencies, toothache, root canal
  • ENT, eye care and general consultations
  • Physiotherapy after an injury
  • Diagnostic tests and lab services
  • Specialist doctors patients look up by name or condition

These patients don't need to be persuaded that they need help. They need to be persuaded that your clinic is the right choice. That's why Google Ads for clinics should be paired with a strong Google Business Profile: reviews, photos, timings and an accurate map location do a lot of the convincing.

The common mistake here is broad keywords. "Hair" or "skin" brings clicks from students, job seekers and people looking for home remedies. Group keywords by intent, use location targeting for the towns you actually serve, and add negative keywords from the first week.

When Meta Ads usually makes more sense

Meta tends to work better for treatments that are planned, elective or emotional:

  • Hair treatments and hair transplant
  • Skin, laser and aesthetic treatments
  • Cosmetic dentistry, aligners and smile design
  • Fertility and IVF
  • Weight management and wellness programmes

People often think about these treatments for months before booking. Meta lets you stay in front of them with educational videos and honest answers to common questions, so when they're ready, your clinic is the one they already trust.

The common mistake here is chasing cheap leads. Instant lead forms can bring a lot of names and numbers very quickly, and many of them never pick up the phone. Adding a qualifying question, sending leads to WhatsApp, or calling within minutes usually matters more than lowering cost per lead.

Google reaches people who are already searching. Meta reaches people who haven't started searching yet.

The rules clinics in India can't ignore

Healthcare advertising comes with extra responsibility. The National Medical Commission (NMC) issued new guidelines on 6 October 2026 that apply to doctors, clinics and hospitals across print, websites, social media, WhatsApp and digital ads. Meta and Google also have their own healthcare ad policies. In practice:

  • Share factual, verifiable information (services, facilities, location, fees) and genuine health education
  • No patient testimonials, reviews or patient stories for promotion, even with consent
  • No before-and-after photos or result showcases
  • No "best", "No.1", "100% success", "painless" or guaranteed-result claims
  • No discounts, limited-time offers, coupons or referral rewards to attract patients
  • No fear-based ads, patient solicitation or influencer promotion

The details, clause by clause, are in my guide to the NMC advertising guidelines 2026.

Meta also removed many health-related interest targeting options a few years ago, so for most clinics, your creative and message now do more of the targeting than interest settings do. Write for the patient you want, and the right people tend to respond.

This is general guidance, not legal advice. Check the latest NMC notices and platform policies before running a campaign.

Before you spend on either: set up tracking

This is the part most clinics skip, and it's why so many say "ads don't work for us". Before launching, make sure you can answer one question: which ad brought which appointment?

  • Google Ads conversion tracking for calls and form submissions
  • Meta Pixel and Conversions API on your website
  • A simple sheet or CRM where your front desk marks which leads actually booked

Without this, you're judging campaigns by clicks and likes. With it, you can compare cost per real appointment across both platforms and move budget to whatever is working.

So, where should your clinic start?

If patients search for what you offer, start with Google and fix your Google Business Profile at the same time. If your treatment needs trust and education, start with Meta and focus on lead quality, not volume. Once one channel is tracked and working, test the other. Most growing clinics end up using both, Google for demand that exists and Meta for demand they create.

FAQ

Is Google Ads or Meta Ads better for a clinic?

Neither is better for every clinic. Google Ads works best for urgent or high-intent treatments that patients actively search for, like "dentist near me". Meta Ads works best for planned or elective treatments where patients need awareness and trust first, like hair transplant, skin treatments or fertility care. Many clinics start with one and add the other once tracking is in place.

How much budget does a clinic need to start ads in Kerala?

There is no fixed number. It depends on the treatment, the city and the competition. Start with a budget you can run consistently for at least four to six weeks so the campaign has enough data to learn, and judge results by cost per genuine appointment, not cost per click.

Can doctors in India advertise on Facebook and Google?

Only within strict limits. Under the NMC guidelines of 6 October 2026, doctors cannot solicit patients or use testimonials, before-and-after photos, discounts, superiority claims or influencer promotion. Hospitals and clinics may share factual information such as services, facilities, location and fees, and educational content is allowed. Meta and Google also have their own healthcare ad policies, so check the full NMC guidelines and your State Medical Council before running ads.
