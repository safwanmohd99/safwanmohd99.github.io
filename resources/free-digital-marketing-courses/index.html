01

A certificate is not a skill

It shows you finished a course. Employers and clients ask what you have done. I did a BA in English and one academy course. Most of what I use today I learned by working on real accounts.

02

Practise while you learn

After each course, do one small real thing: write a blog post, set up Google Search Console, or run a small ad for a shop you know. Keep screenshots. That is your portfolio.

03

One at a time

Starting four courses together usually means finishing none. Finish one, then open the next.

04

Check the course page

These platforms change their courses, hours and certificate rules often. What you see on the official page is the latest. If a link here stops working, tell me and I will fix it.

Questions

The straight answers.

Are these digital marketing courses really free?

The lessons in all six are free to take. The one thing to know is Meta Blueprint: its online lessons are free, but the official Meta Certified exams have a fee. You don't need to pay for anything on this list to learn.

Which course should I start with as a beginner?

Start with Google's Fundamentals of Digital Marketing. It covers a little of everything. After that, take the HubSpot course, then pick one direction: SEO or paid ads.

Will a certificate get me a digital marketing job?

A certificate shows you finished a course. It helps on a resume, but employers and clients mostly ask what you have actually done. So practise while you learn: run a small ad, write a blog post, or grow one page, and show that work.

Do I have to finish all six courses?

No. Pick by your goal. For paid ads, do Google Skillshop and Meta Blueprint. For SEO, do the Google fundamentals course and then Semrush Academy. Finishing two courses and practising is better than collecting six certificates.

Are the courses in English?

The course pages are in English, and some platforms offer other languages for some courses. Check the language option on each course page.
