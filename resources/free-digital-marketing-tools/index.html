Part 1 · SEO tools
Tool 01 of 15 · Google

Google Search Console

See which keywords your site ranks for Free

Search Console shows which keywords your website ranks for on Google, how many clicks it gets, and what technical issues the site has. It is the first tool to set up for SEO.

Open Google Search Console

What you can use it for

  • See the search terms people used before they reached your site
  • Check which pages Google has indexed and which it has not
  • Submit your sitemap and ask Google to index a new page

Who it is for

Anyone with a website.

Good to know: You have to verify that you own the site, and data takes a few days to start showing.

Tool 02 of 15 · Microsoft

Bing Webmaster Tools

Search Console, but for Bing Free

Like Google Search Console, but for Bing search. You can check how your site performs on Bing, and you can import your website straight from Google Search Console, so setup is quick.

Open Bing Webmaster Tools

What you can use it for

  • See clicks, impressions and keywords from Bing
  • Import your site from Search Console instead of verifying again
  • Submit your sitemap so Bing finds your pages

Who it is for

Anyone who has already set up Search Console. It takes a few minutes.

Good to know: For many sites, Bing sends less traffic than Google. It is still worth doing because setup is so easy.

Tool 03 of 15 · Screaming Frog

Screaming Frog SEO Spider

A full technical SEO audit of your website Free

A tool for a complete SEO audit of a website. It finds broken links, missing page titles, duplicate pages and many other technical SEO issues. The free version crawls up to 500 URLs.

Open Screaming Frog SEO Spider

What you can use it for

  • Find broken links (404 pages)
  • Spot pages with a missing or duplicate title, meta description or H1
  • Export the full page list to Excel or Google Sheets

Who it is for

People who audit small and medium websites. If your site has fewer than 500 URLs, the free version is enough.

Good to know: This is software you install on your computer, not a website. Sites with more than 500 URLs need the paid licence.

↑ Back to the list of 15

Tool 04 of 15 · Neil Patel

Ubersuggest

Keyword research and competitor keywords Free

Ubersuggest helps with keyword research, shows search volume, and lets you see which keywords your competitors use. The free version has limited searches.

Open Ubersuggest

What you can use it for

  • Check the search volume and difficulty of a keyword
  • Get new keyword ideas related to it
  • See which keywords a competitor website ranks for

Who it is for

People choosing blog topics and keywords for service pages.

Good to know: The free version limits how many searches you can do per day. Search volume numbers are estimates, not exact.

↑ Back to the list of 15

Tool 05 of 15 · AnswerThePublic

AnswerThePublic

Find what your audience is asking Free

AnswerThePublic shows what your audience is asking about a topic. That makes it very useful for blog topics, content ideas and Reels. Usage is limited in the free version.

Open AnswerThePublic

What you can use it for

  • Type a keyword and get questions people ask (what, how, why, can)
  • Use those questions as blog headings or FAQs
  • Get topic ideas for Reels and YouTube videos

Who it is for

Anyone who makes content: blogs, Reels, YouTube.

Good to know: Free usage is limited. The questions come from search suggestions, so you still decide which ones are worth answering.

Part 2 · Ads research tools
Tool 06 of 15 · Meta

Meta Ad Library

See competitors' Facebook and Instagram ads Free

See which ads your competitors are running on Facebook and Instagram. You can study their creatives, offers and ad messaging. It is the best place to research competitor ads.

Open Meta Ad Library

What you can use it for

  • Search a brand's Page name to see its active ads
  • Look at the images, videos, copy and buttons to see which offer they push
  • Check the date an ad started running

Who it is for

Anyone running Meta Ads, or planning ads for a new business.

Good to know: For most ads it does not show how much was spent or what results came. Use it for ideas, and do not copy ads as they are.

Tool 07 of 15 · Google

Google Ads Transparency Center

See ads brands run on Google and YouTube Free

Google's own ad library. You can check the ads that different brands show on Google Search, YouTube and other Google platforms.

Open Google Ads Transparency Center

What you can use it for

  • Search an advertiser name or website to see its ads
  • Filter by ad format: text, image or video
  • Pick a country or region to see ads shown there

Who it is for

People running Google Ads, to see what ad copy competitors use.

Good to know: Results such as clicks and cost are not shown here either. Set the region filter correctly or the ads you want may not appear.

Tool 08 of 15 · LinkedIn

LinkedIn Ad Library

B2B competitor ads on LinkedIn Free

A tool B2B marketers should look at. You can study the ads, creatives and messaging your competitors run on LinkedIn.

Open LinkedIn Ad Library

What you can use it for

  • Search a company name or keyword to see ads
  • See who B2B competitors are talking to, and which offer and message they use
  • Get ideas for lead magnets, webinars and case study ads

Who it is for

People who market B2B service companies.

Good to know: Performance data such as leads and cost is not shown. Use it to study the message, not the results.

Tool 09 of 15 · Google Ads

Google Keyword Planner

Keyword research before you start ads Free

Use it for keyword research before you start a Google Ads campaign. For each keyword you get estimated search volume, competition and CPC estimates.

Open Google Keyword Planner

What you can use it for

  • Discover new keyword ideas
  • See roughly how many people search a keyword each month
  • Estimate the cost per click (CPC) you may pay

Who it is for

People planning a Google Ads campaign, and people doing SEO keyword research.

Good to know: You need a Google Ads account to use it. If you are not running ads, it may show ranges instead of exact numbers. Everything is an estimate.

Tool 10 of 15 · Google

Google Trends

Is interest in a topic rising or falling? Free

Google Trends shows whether search interest in a topic is going up or down, and which location has the most interest. You can even look at search trends in Kerala.

Open Google Trends

What you can use it for

  • Compare two or more keywords
  • Change the location from India to Kerala to see the local trend
  • Check related queries for new content ideas

Who it is for

People planning a content calendar or seasonal and festival campaigns.

Good to know: The 0 to 100 number is not the count of searches. It only compares interest over time or between topics.

Part 3 · Analytics, performance and design
Tool 11 of 15 · Google

Google Analytics 4

Who visits your site and what they do Free

Google Analytics 4 tracks who visits your website, where they come from and what they do on it.

Open Google Analytics 4

What you can use it for

  • See how many visitors you get and from which source (Google, Instagram, direct)
  • See which pages people view most
  • Track actions like form submits and WhatsApp clicks as key events

Who it is for

Any business that wants enquiries from its website.

Good to know: You have to add the GA4 code to your site, and you only get data from the day you add it. Because it collects visitor data, mention it in your privacy policy.

Tool 12 of 15 · Microsoft

Microsoft Clarity

Heatmaps and session recordings, free Free

Clarity shows where visitors click, where they scroll and which part of the page they spend the most time on, using heatmaps and session recordings. You can use it for free.

Open Microsoft Clarity

What you can use it for

  • See which buttons get the most clicks and which sections nobody reads
  • Watch recordings to see how visitors use your site
  • Find where people drop off on your contact form

Who it is for

People who want more enquiries from a landing page or contact page.

Good to know: You have to add the Clarity code to your site. It records visitors, so update your privacy policy or cookie notice, and use its setting to mask what people type in forms.

Tool 13 of 15 · Google

Looker Studio

All your data on one dashboard Free

Looker Studio shows data from different platforms, such as Google Analytics, Search Console and Google Ads, in one dashboard. It is very useful for client reporting.

Open Looker Studio

What you can use it for

  • Connect GA4, Search Console and Google Ads into one report
  • Start from a ready-made template and change what you need
  • Share a live link with your client instead of sending a new PDF every month

Who it is for

People who send monthly reports to clients or a team.

Good to know: The data has to exist first in GA4, Search Console and so on, and you need access to those accounts. Check who can view a report before you share it.

Tool 14 of 15 · Google

PageSpeed Insights

How fast is your site, especially on mobile? Free

PageSpeed Insights tells you how fast your website is, whether it has loading problems on mobile, and how to improve its performance.

Open PageSpeed Insights

What you can use it for

  • Enter a page URL to get a mobile and desktop speed report
  • Check Core Web Vitals (LCP, INP, CLS)
  • Get suggestions on what to change to make the page faster

Who it is for

Website owners, and people who audit client sites.

Good to know: The score changes a little each time you test. Look at the mobile result, and do not chase one score.

Canva

Posts, ad creatives and thumbnails, fast Free

Canva lets you create social media posts, ad creatives, presentations, thumbnails and many other designs very easily. You can use it even if you are not a professional designer.

How to start

Four steps, in this order.

Don't try all 15 at once. Take one tool at a time, with one goal.

  1. Set up Search Console and GA4

    If you have a website, do these two first. Data takes time to build up, so start early.

  2. Check your site and its speed

    Run Screaming Frog to find broken links and missing titles, then test your pages in PageSpeed Insights.

  3. Study keywords and competitors

    Use Keyword Planner, Ubersuggest, Google Trends, AnswerThePublic and the three ad libraries to see what people search for and what competitors say.

  4. Report, improve and design

    Build a report in Looker Studio, watch visitors in Clarity to improve the site, and make your creatives in Canva.

Questions

Free marketing tools: your questions.

What are the best free digital marketing tools?

For SEO: Google Search Console, Bing Webmaster Tools, Screaming Frog, Ubersuggest and AnswerThePublic. For ads research: Meta Ad Library, Google Ads Transparency Center, LinkedIn Ad Library, Google Keyword Planner and Google Trends. For analytics, performance and design: Google Analytics 4, Microsoft Clarity, Looker Studio, PageSpeed Insights and Canva. All 15 are on this page with their official links.

Are these tools really free?

You can start with all of them for free. Search Console, Bing Webmaster Tools, Meta Ad Library, Google Ads Transparency Center, LinkedIn Ad Library, Google Trends, GA4, Clarity, Looker Studio and PageSpeed Insights are free to use. Screaming Frog (up to 500 URLs), Ubersuggest, AnswerThePublic and Canva have limits on the free version, and you need a paid plan for more. Keyword Planner needs a Google Ads account.

Which free SEO tool should I set up first?

Google Search Console. It shows which keywords your website appears for on Google, how many clicks it gets and whether your pages are indexed. After that, set up Bing Webmaster Tools, then audit your site with Screaming Frog.

How can I see my competitors' ads for free?

Use Meta Ad Library for Facebook and Instagram ads, Google Ads Transparency Center for Google Search and YouTube ads, and LinkedIn Ad Library for B2B ads. Search the brand name to see the ads it runs. For most ads these libraries do not show how much was spent or what results came.

How do I do keyword research for free?

Use Google Keyword Planner, Ubersuggest (limited searches on the free version) and AnswerThePublic. Use Google Trends to compare which keyword is gaining interest. Search volume numbers are estimates, so do not rely on one tool alone.

How do I check my website speed for free?

Enter the page URL in PageSpeed Insights. You get mobile and desktop speed, Core Web Vitals and suggestions to improve the page. Pay most attention to the mobile result.

What is the difference between Google Analytics and Search Console?

Search Console covers what happens before people reach your site: which keywords you appeared for on Google and how many clicked. Google Analytics 4 covers what happens after they arrive: which pages they viewed and what they did. You need both.

Can I get a digital marketing job by learning these tools?

Knowing the tools helps, but employers and clients mostly look at what you have done with them. Set up Search Console for a small website, write up a competitor ads study, or build a Looker Studio report, and show that work. If you are starting out, see my list of 5 free digital marketing courses.
