Case study

How a Kerala allergy clinic network got a 6.6x return

Allergy Asthma Foundation runs outpatient allergy and asthma clinics across Kerala, including Kochi, Kozhikode, Thrissur, Palakkad, Perinthalmanna and Mannarkkad. I have run their Meta Ads for about six months.

We started with click-to-WhatsApp ads. They brought plenty of chats, but too few became patients, and busy clinic staff spent time on enquiries that went nowhere. So we changed four things.

Lead forms

Ads opened a short Instant Form instead of a WhatsApp chat. The extra few seconds filter out casual clickers.

Qualifying questions

Who the visit is for, the main concern, when they plan to consult, and the nearest location, with conditional logic.

One campaign per location

Each clinic location got its own campaign and its own budget.

Leads tracked to revenue

Every lead went into a shared sheet with the clinic team until it became a visit or was closed.

Lead forms or WhatsApp ads: which works for clinics?

Both. Lead forms and click-to-WhatsApp ads can work for clinics and hospitals, and different branches can use different ones. For the allergy clinics, lead forms were the right fit. For other clinics and hospitals, WhatsApp ads work just as well. What decides the result is not the ad type but what happens in the first minutes after someone enquires. That is where WhatsApp automation comes in.

Why WhatsApp automation matters, even with lead forms

Someone who fills a form at 9 pm and hears nothing until the next afternoon has often moved on. With automation, they get a WhatsApp message within seconds, for example:

  • A thank-you that confirms the enquiry, with the clinic or hospital name
  • The address, map link and timings of the branch they chose
  • A simple way to pick a time or ask a question, right there in WhatsApp
  • A note that the team will call, so the call is expected and picked up

For WhatsApp ads, the automated first reply asks the same qualifying questions a form would, with tap-to-answer buttons, so your staff only step in once the person has said what they need and where. Business-initiated WhatsApp messages are charged by Meta; I explain the charges in WhatsApp Business charges from October 2026.

Good qualifying questions, in a form or a chat

Keep them multiple choice, so they take seconds to answer and give your team clean data:

  • Who is the visit for? Tells the team who to expect.
  • What is the main concern? Tells staff what the person needs before they call.
  • When are you planning to visit? The biggest filter: someone "just looking" is followed up differently.
  • Which branch is nearest? Sends the enquiry to the right location, never the wrong city.

What can't clinics show in Meta Ads in India?

Two sets of rules apply: the NMC guidelines for doctors, clinics and hospitals, and Meta's own advertising standards. Here is the short version I work with.

AllowedNot allowed
Services and treatments you offerPatient testimonials and reviews in ads
Facilities and locationBefore-and-after photos
Fees and timingsDiscounts, offers and "free consultation" hooks
Genuine health education (signs, tests, when to see a doctor)"Best", "No.1" or other superiority claims
Contact details and how to bookInfluencer promotion and AI-generated promotional campaigns

Meta adds its own limits. You cannot target people by health condition (Meta removed those targeting options in January 2022), and ad text must not suggest you know something about the viewer's health. "Do you have asthma?" breaks Meta's personal attributes rule; "Allergy tests at our Palakkad clinic" does not. Lead forms also collect health information, so it should be shared only with the clinic staff who need it. My NMC advertising guidelines 2026 guide covers the details.

FAQ

Meta Ads for clinics, answered.

Do Meta Ads work for clinics in Kerala?

Yes, when they are judged by patient visits, not by cheap leads. For a network of outpatient allergy clinics across Kerala, ₹49,634 of Meta Ads spend brought ₹3,28,100 in revenue in September 2026, about a 6.6x return, using lead forms with qualifying questions, one campaign per clinic location and tracking every lead to a visit.

How much should a clinic spend on Meta Ads?

There is no single number, because every clinic is different: city, treatments, number of branches and competition all change it. I suggest a starting budget after looking at your clinic, and we judge it by visits and revenue, not cost per lead. Your ad budget is paid directly to Meta and is separate from my fee.

Do lead forms or WhatsApp ads work better for clinics?

Both can work for clinics and hospitals, and different branches can use different ones. The key is WhatsApp automation: an instant reply that asks qualifying questions for WhatsApp ads, and an instant WhatsApp message the moment a lead form is submitted, so nobody waits for a callback.

What can't clinics show in Meta Ads under the NMC rules?

Under the NMC advertising guidelines issued on 6 October 2026, doctors and clinics cannot use patient testimonials, before-and-after photos, discounts or offers, superiority claims such as best or No.1, influencer promotion or AI-generated promotional campaigns. Factual information about services, facilities, location and fees, and genuine health education, are allowed.

Can I target people with a health condition on Facebook?

No. Meta removed detailed targeting options linked to health topics in January 2022, and its rules do not allow ad text that suggests you know something about the viewer's health, such as asking "Do you have asthma?". Clinic ads now rely on location targeting, a clear message and good lead form questions.

Should my clinic start with Meta Ads or Google Ads?

Start with Google Ads if patients already search for your treatment when they need it, such as dental pain, ENT or diagnostics. Start with Meta Ads for planned treatments that need trust before booking, such as allergy, skin, hair or women's health. Set up tracking first either way.
